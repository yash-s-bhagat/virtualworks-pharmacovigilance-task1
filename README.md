## ADR Identification Basics

**Intern:** Yash Bhagat  
**Domain:** Pharmacovigilance  
**Organization:** VirtualWorks Lab  

## Objective

To analyze a patient case scenario and identify a potential Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) by reviewing the patient's symptoms, medication history and timeline.

## Patient Scenario

- Patient: Female
- Age: 45 years
- Presenting symptoms:
  - Rash
  - Itching
  - Nausea
  - Dizziness

## ADR Assessment

The reported symptoms occurring after medication exposure may represent a suspected adverse drug reaction.

The symptoms were reviewed in relation to the patient's medication exposure and the timing of symptom onset.

### Potential ADR

The main suspected adverse reactions are:

- Skin rash
- Itching
- Nausea
- Dizziness

### Assessment

The temporal relationship between medication exposure and the appearance of symptoms should be reviewed to determine whether the suspected medicine is likely to be responsible.

Other possible causes, concomitant medicines, medical history and alternative explanations should also be considered before establishing causality.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates the basic process of ADR identification in pharmacovigilance:

1. Review the patient information.
2. Identify the reported symptoms.
3. Review medication history.
4. Establish the timeline between drug exposure and symptoms.
5. Identify the suspected drug.
6. Consider alternative causes.
7. Document the suspected ADR appropriately.

## Key Learning

This task helped me understand the importance of systematic assessment of patient symptoms, medication history and timelines when identifying suspected ADRs.
